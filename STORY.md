# ARARAT — Redemption and Self-Exile

The flood has ended. An unnamed survivor has reached dry land, but cannot call it home. Survival feels like a sentence whose meaning has not yet been spoken. This is an original fictional voice, not a quotation from scripture or a claim about a historical passenger.

The ark is a place of shelter and memory. The small boat is a second departure, chosen rather than imposed. The story does not promise that exile cures guilt. It asks whether redemption can begin with choosing how to carry the life that remains.

## The playable sequence

The opening is a short handwritten passage floating over the cold shore: “The water withdrew. The names did not.” The player then walks freely through the environment.

Three memories can be discovered in any order:

- **The olive branch on the shore stone.** Life has returned, but the survivor realizes that being spared is not the same as being forgiven.
- **The linen and offering table inside the ark.** The voyage carried the body ashore while leaving part of the survivor at sea. Empty places and small acts of care hold the memory of loss.
- **The knotted rope inside the ark.** Knots stand for names the survivor fears forgetting. Redemption may be what one chooses not to abandon, rather than a destination.

Each memory floats directly over the scene in a thick, rough brush hand. Passages fade in slowly, remain in place while read, then fade away before the next words appear. There is no vertical slide, parchment, scroll or dialogue box. There are no character labels, menu panels, quest counters or modern buttons. Click the prose, or use E, Space or Enter, to continue after a dissolve finishes. E near a memory recalls it; some memories surface automatically when approached closely.

The shore boat cannot depart before all three memories have been explored. An early attempt produces a narrative response rather than a system warning: the names are still knotted inside. After the last memory, the survivor remembers the little boat at the waterline.

At the boat, the survivor considers staying and calling the shore the promised land. Instead, they take one oar, one bundle and an olive cutting. The last rope is left untied.

The camera boards the boat. It moves out into the open water as the light changes from cold grey to orange-red. The setting sun leaves a broken golden path on the sea. The ark and mountains remain behind the departing survivor.

## The ending

“We never arrived at the promised land.”

There is a world washed clean, and a life the survivor has not yet learned to inhabit. The survivor calls the departure exile. For the first time, it does not feel like drowning.

After the final passage disappears, the sunset holds for a few quiet seconds. The image then fades slowly to full black. Camera, boat, light and memories reset while hidden; the cold opening returns through another slow dissolve, followed by the first handwritten passage. The cycle has no victory screen or modern credits panel, and does not reload the 3D scene.

## Visual language

The cold exterior, dark worn cedar and grey ochre sediment preserve the aftermath of catastrophe. Olive leaves and sparse first growth hold a restrained sense of life. The final orange-red sky and gold-lit water suggest warmth without declaring that the survivor has found home.

Caveat Brush is a broad, irregular handwritten face. Its warm, slightly broken strokes float over the image, with restrained shadows for legibility and no background panel. Font and story assets are local; the experience does not require an online font service. All visible prose is English. The definitive in-game passages are in `src/story.js`.
